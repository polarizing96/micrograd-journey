The nudge: how the slope gets measured

Set the knob anywhere, then press Poke. Watch the knob move a tiny bit, the output react, and the slope fall out of one division. (Like a speedometer: distance moved ÷ tiny time.)

What just happened: a slope is a rate — "how much output per 1 unit of knob." To measure it you poke the knob a tiny bit, see how much the output reacts, and divide: slope = (output moved) ÷ (knob moved). That's the nudge. Nothing more.

Try poking at different knob settings. At knob = 2 the slope is big and positive (steep uphill to the right). At knob = 0 it's ~0 (flat — poking barely changes the output). At knob = −2 it's negative (uphill to the left). The nudge re-measures the slope wherever you currently are — just like a speedometer reads your speed right now, not your average for the trip.

The payoff: that slope number is the steepness + direction of the downhill arrow from the rolling-ball demo. Nudge to measure it → then step downhill to use it.
